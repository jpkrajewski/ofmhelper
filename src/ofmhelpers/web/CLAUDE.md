# web

FastAPI app ("Ascend Global LLC — Content Ops"): users who sign in with Google, Apple or email + password (`ofmhelpers.integrations.signin`), each owning their own phones/accounts/reels/jobs/files/API keys (admins too: nobody sees another person's), AI generation, media download/cleaning, landing-page CRM, file manager. Jinja2 pages + a small vanilla-JS polling layer.

## Users and ownership (2026-10-22)
- `users` (+ `user_identities`, `password_links`, `user_api_keys`; alembic c2d3e4f5a6b7). Roles: admin (/users + admin tools), user, and since 2026-11-11 va (below). The old shared-password VA of before 2026-10 is gone: its jobs and the admin's belong to the password admin (email "admin").
- Owned (`user_id`, ON DELETE RESTRICT, alembic d3e4f5a6b7c8): devices, logins, tracked_accounts, creators, watched_profiles (PK user_id+username; a username is scanned once, written to every owner's row), watched_profile_groups. Uniques are per owner (two users may both have "Phone 01" or track one handle; a login never links across owners, so nobody reveals another's password). Drive folders follow the login; the sweep shares each with its owner's Google account (`shared_with`). A user who owns rows cannot be deleted: deactivate.
- **Roles, access and who a request acts as (2026-11-11).** Three roles: admin, user, va (`schemas/users.py` `Role`). THE access map is `schemas/access.py`: `Area` (a page with its routes) and `Action` (one thing inside a page) -> the roles that may; a new page is one `Area` + its line, a role change is one edit there. Every router names its area (`dependencies=[Access(Area.X)]`, `web/dependencies.py`), a narrower route adds `Can(Action.Y)`; the menu (`schemas/nav.py`, each `NavItem.area`), the templates (`can(request, "drive-upload")`) and the tests' page lists (`tests/app_pages.py`) read the same map; `tests/unit/architecture/test_access.py` fails on a route with no area. The auth middleware builds one `Viewer` per request (`features/users/viewing.py`, kept on `request.state.viewer`): `user` (who the pages are for), `user_ids` (whose jobs they see: their own and their VAs'), `keys_of` (whose saved keys pay), `by` (the admin looking, when "view as" is on). `CurrentUser` / `Actor` / `Actors` / `OwnerId` / `Scope` / `AssetsRoot` / `Viewing` are views of it; templates read `viewer_of(request)`, never the session.
- **VA** (`users.owner_id`, alembic d2e3f4a5b6c8; set on /users): opens Home, Gen AI, Clone, Download, Edit, Account (password only). Runs on the OWNER's saved API keys (`Keys.of` maps a VA to the owner; the key field is not rendered, no key reaches the VA's browser; a VA with no owner has no key, never an .env one; a VA's run saves no key). Files land in the owner's part of the asset store (`assets_root_for_user`). The owner sees the VA's jobs, the VA only their own. No Drive upload, no saved keys (`Action`s). A VA cannot own a VA and always has an employer (the owner; changeable on /users, never cleared). **A role is picked when the user is added and never changes** (owner's rule 2026-11-12: no role control on /users, no route takes one; a wrong pick = deactivate or remove, add again).
- **A VA signs in with "Login as Virtual Assistant" only** (2026-11-12; `/login/va`, public, `va_login.html`, button on /login): the EMPLOYER's email + the password the admin typed for that VA when adding them on /users (the add form shows "VA's employer" and "VA password" only when the role is VA; no set-password link; a VA row has "Set password" instead of "Password link"). `features/signin/credentials.py` `va_sign_in(employer_email, password)`: the employer's active VA whose password it is; so a VA's password only has to be unique among that employer's VAs (`set_va_password` refuses a clash, min length `OFM_PASSWORD_MIN_LENGTH`), two employers' VAs may share one, and it opens nothing else: the email form refuses a VA, Big G Login stays the admin password alone, a VA links no Google/Apple account and changes no password on /account (`Action.CHANGE_PASSWORD`). The employer's own password, the admin password and a user's are refused on the VA page. Failures are counted and braked like every login. A VA moved to another employer signs in with that employer's email.
- **View as** (admins, /users "View as" -> `POST /users/{id}/viewing`; banner "Viewing as <name>" + "Back to my view" -> `POST /viewing/delete`): the admin's requests become that person's `Viewer`, role included, so they see exactly that person's menu and pages. Look only: the middleware refuses every method but GET/HEAD/OPTIONS in one place (403 "Viewing as <name>: look only"), except leaving the view and logging out. No key of the viewed person is sent (pre-fills empty, /account shows tails). The session holds only the viewed id; each request re-checks that the admin is still an admin and the person still active, else the view ends. The span carries `viewing.id`.
- **Jobs belong to a user row (2026-11-11, alembic e3f4a5b6c7d9).** `jobs.user_id` (FK users, RESTRICT, index `(user_id, created_at)`) replaced the text `jobs.actor`; `Job.actor` is derived (`str(user_id)`), so worker kwargs, Redis keys and the action log are unchanged. `create_job(..., actor=None)` is still the agency's; a non-numeric actor is nobody's. READS take `Readers` (one actor or `Viewer.actors`: status, file, preview, poster, galleries as `gallery.page(viewer)`); WRITES take the person's own `Actor`. A card of someone else's job (a VA's, seen by the owner) ends its meta line with the maker's name (`JobCard.made_by`); only the maker hides a card (`hidden_at` is one column: a card the VA hides leaves the owner's gallery too). The owner uploads a VA's clone to Drive (`queue_uploads(..., readers)`); the VA cannot. A user with any job, a sign-in event included, cannot be deleted any more, only deactivated. Dev prod-snapshot at the migration: 1277 jobs, none without a real user.
- **Cost is a column (same revision).** `jobs.cost_usd` NUMERIC(12,4) + `jobs.cost_estimated`; `meta` keeps the per-request breakdown only. `features/jobs/cost.py` `cost_of`: the sum of what the provider reported (kie, Elyum); when it reported nothing and the run is a clone (`clone`, `clone_links`, `clone_long`: provider, resolution and source length in its params), an estimate from the /clone price table (`features/clone/targets.json` + the `settings.web` per-second prices), flagged estimated and scaled by the pieces that ended done; anything else NULL = no known price, never 0 (/generate and /pictures on APIYI, Higgsfield, RunningHub).
- **/spending** (`Area.SPENDING`, More menu; admin and users, a VA gets 403): per VA of the reader (an admin: every VA under its owner) today / last 7 days / this month: runs, USD, "est." where estimated, "N runs without a price". Days in the reader's `ofm_timezone`. A run = a job that kept at least one provider request. One grouped SQL query (`JobRepository.spend`); it filters on `meta->'generations'`, the one place a JSONB filter is left.
- **Downloads are kept a short time, then a poster and the link (2026-11-11, alembic f4b5c6d7e8a1; detail in `routers/downloads/CLAUDE.md`).** Table `downloads`: one row per downloaded file (owner, job, link, position, file and asset-store path, `stored`, `last_used_at`); the job row stays the run's record. The same link pasted again downloads nothing and its card moves first (`/download-assets` orders by newest `last_used_at`); a hidden card pasted again is unhidden. Sweep `downloads cleanup` (hourly, `previews` queue, production only unless `OFM_DOWNLOADS_SWEEP`; `features/downloads/cleanup.py`, shaped like the trials one): a video no job used goes after 24 h, one a job used 14 days after its last use, anything after 30 days; nothing asked for or used within the last day is taken; the poster is made first and stays with the row. `./uploads` and `./downloads` are two mounts, so every download was on disk twice (asset-store copy): both go. A removed card shows its poster and one Download button -> `POST /download-media/{job_id}/replays` re-runs the SAME job (`Restart.replay`, no restart counter; links whose files are there are skipped; a double click queues once); `/files/{job}/{i}` of a removed video answers 410. Clone Recreate: `/refs/missing` also answers `restorable`, and the picker fetches the video again ("Downloading again…") instead of dropping it. Dry count before a first round: `python -m ofmhelpers.features.downloads.cleanup` in the app container (writes nothing). Dev 2026-11-11 on the prod snapshot's rows: 108 due by the 24 h rule, 137 by the 14 day rule, 0 by the 30 day rule (oldest download 2026-10-17), of 388. Known gap: a link pasted again while its first download still runs downloads twice.
- **Everyone sees and acts on only their own data, admins included** (2026-10-22). An admin's extra power is managing people (/users) and the admin-only tools (file manager, action log, applications, character sheet), never reading another user's phones/accounts/reels/jobs/Drive/files.
- Requests: `AuthMiddleware.scope(request)` (always the user's own id) for reads/writes on existing rows, `owner_id(request)` for new rows, `actor(request)` for jobs (actor = user id string). Store `owner=None` ("everyone's") is for system work only -- the sweeps (`sweeps.py`: drive, reel_watch, account_tracker, recovery); no request passes it. Stores take `owner`/`user_id`; a store call without one acts as the agency (`stores.users.agency_id()` = password admin).
- Assets: everyone `assets/users/<id>/`; the agency (password account) keeps the store root, where its files predate per-user folders, minus `users/` (`in_asset_root`: listing, serving and reuse checks).
- API keys: /account saves a user's own per provider key slot (encrypted; `Provider.key_slots`: one per provider, APIYI one per token group -- `apiyi`, `apiyi-wan`, `apiyi-seedance`; the slot id is `user_api_keys.provider`); forms pre-fill one; an admin with none falls back to the .env key; a user never gets the .env key. Which key a run goes out on is decided at the submit, in one place (2026-11-11, `features/account/keys.py` `Keys.for_run(provider, model, typed)`, called by all five submits): a typed key that is none of theirs runs as typed; otherwise their keys in order (the model's slot, their default slot, the rest, then for an admin the .env keys), the first one the provider says belongs to the model's slot (`Provider.slot_of_key`; APIYI reads the key's token group from its model list, so a key saved under the wrong slot is passed over: prod 2026-11-11, apiyi/CLAUDE.md), else the first; none = "API key is required". A run that succeeded on a key its person had not saved for that slot saves it (`keep_used`, called by `run_job`; never a .env key): the first outside user typed his key on every run. `jobs.meta.generations[].key_tail` = the last 4 characters of the key each request ran on (what /account shows).
- Cookies (2026-10-22): `/cookies` (`routers/cookies.py`, every user, More menu) uploads/replaces/removes the user's OWN cookies.txt: `user_cookies` (alembic b1c2d3e4f5a7, `EncryptedText`, PK user_id), `stores/cookies.py` (rows only; repository caches nothing) + `features/cookies/jar.py` (the upload's checks, what the page shows). The page shows file name, upload time, domains, cookie count, Instagram login + expiry -- never a value. Whose cookies work uses: a download job = whoever started it (`user_id` kwarg, cookies read in the worker, never in RQ/Redis); a tracked account's scan = its owner; a watched profile's single shared scan = the earliest-added watcher who has cookies, else none (`for_watched`, the only cross-user use; the value never leaves the worker). Upload queues `unlock_gated(user_id)` (their watched profiles' login-only reels) and `rescan_limited(user_id)` (their limited accounts). The old shared `OFM_COOKIES_FILE` became the password admin's by the revision (file left in place; `migrate` mounts `cookies_data` read-only).

**New background-job tool?** Reuse `features/jobs/runner.py` (`start_job`) + `stores/jobs.py` + `routers/task_helpers/` + one router file + its run function in `features/<page>/`; no new plumbing.

## Layout
`main.py` (assembly only), `api_keys.py`, `phones_workbook.py`, `middleware/`, `recovery.py`, `reel_watch.py`, `account_tracker.py`, `drive_folders.py`, `ref_usage.py`, `schemas/`, `templates_config.py`, `stores/`, `db/`, `routers/`, `templates/`, `static/`.

## Sessions and View as (2026-11-14)
- **The session slides.** Starlette re-sends the cookie only when the session is written, and nothing wrote it after sign-in: it ended 5 h after sign-in whatever the person did (owner: "user is getting logged out when using the app"). `AuthMiddleware` now writes `seen` on every user action (a page navigation, any non-read method), so the cookie lives `session_max_age_s` (5 h) from the last action; a script's GET poll does not count. `session.js` sends `PUT /session` (204) on a click or key press, at most once a minute, and moves its own deadline on the answer. No hard maximum on purpose (owner: 5 h, renewed by every action). The cookie is the whole session (signed, HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax, `Secure` when `SESSION_HTTPS_ONLY`), so a copy of it works while it is used.
- **Logout ends every session of the person** (owner 2026-11-14; before, a copy of the cookie outlived Logout). `users.sessions_ended_at` (alembic c8d9e0f1a2b4) is set by Logout (`features/signin/outcome.signed_out`); a session carries when it signed in (`since`) and the middleware refuses one from before that moment ("logged out"), on every device. A password change ends the others too (`credential_stamp`). Sessions from before the deploy have no `since`: they go at their person's first Logout.
- **View as an own VA acts.** `Viewer.acts`: the admin viewing as a VA whose `owner_id` is the admin may do what the VA may (the run is the VA's, on the admin's keys). Anyone else stays look only: 403 "You are viewing as <name>. Go back to your view to do this." Phone passwords and the export are never shown under View as (inventory/CLAUDE.md).
- **A page that is not theirs goes home (2026-11-14).** `AuthMiddleware.require`: a GET page load (`sec-fetch-mode: navigate`) of an area the role may not open (a VA on /accounts, anyone on a retired page) answers 303 to /home, not `{"detail":"Not available to you"}`; a fetch and every write keep the 403. The same for an address no route matches (`web/exceptions.py` `http_error`: /logs answered `{"detail":"Not Found"}`); a route's own 404 stays.
- A card of a VA's job shows the owner "Done by VA: <name>" on its own line under the meta line (`_gallery_card.html`, `[data-made-by]`; generation.js `keepMaker` carries it onto the card redrawn when the run ends).
